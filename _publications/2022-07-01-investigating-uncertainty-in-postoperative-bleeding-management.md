title: "Investigating Uncertainty in Postoperative Bleeding Management: Design Principles for Decision Support"
abstract: >
  Decision-making under uncertainty is a difficult and unavoidable challenge in
  clinical contexts. Technologies such as probabilistic programming languages
  (PPLs) allow their users to explicitly model and reason with uncertainty. By
  taking a user-centric approach to the deployment of these technologies, we
  believe there is an opportunity to involve clinicians in the modelling
  process. In this paper, we present a field study of decisions taken to manage
  postoperative bleeding. From analysis of the findings, we outline three
  central themes that emerge and discuss implications for design, developing a
  set of evaluative design principles to assess a PPL-based tool in this
  context. These include visualising zones of optimal intervention, surfacing
  relative risk trade-offs between teams, and accessing specialist views within
  a holistic picture. These findings provide a structure for critically
  exploring PPL-based tools to support clinical reasoning under uncertainty.
