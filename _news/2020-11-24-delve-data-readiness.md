On 24 November 2020 DELVE published [*Data Readiness: Lessons from an Emergency*](https://rs-delve.github.io/reports/2020/11/24/data-readiness-lessons-from-an-emergency.html). The report asks how happenstance data — from phones, payments, sensors and other digital by-products — can inform crisis decisions when anonymised, aggregated and calibrated, and what institutional gaps the pandemic exposed.

Supporting technical material includes [*Organisational Data Maturity*](https://rs-delve.github.io/addenda/2020/11/24/organizational-data-maturity.html), prepared for DELVE by Neil Lawrence, [Jessica Montgomery](/people/jessica-montgomery.html) and Ulrich Paquet. That addendum proposes a five-level maturity model — reactive, repeatable, managed/integrated, optimized, transparent — with indicators for organisations that want better data management. It builds on Neil’s earlier [Data Readiness Levels](https://inverseprobability.com/publications/data-readiness-levels.html) framing.

The report is among the clearest links between DELVE’s emergency work and the lab’s later agenda on data infrastructure and [AI adoption](/projects/ai-adoption.html). Programme context is on the [DELVE project page](/projects/delve-data-evaluation-and-learning-for-viral-epidemics.html).
