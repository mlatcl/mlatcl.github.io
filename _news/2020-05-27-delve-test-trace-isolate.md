On 27 May 2020 DELVE published [*Test, Trace, Isolate*](https://rs-delve.github.io/reports/2020/05/27/test-trace-isolate.html). The Royal Society’s overview stresses that success depends on [speed, compliance and monitoring](https://royalsociety.org/news/2020/05/success-of-test-trace-and-isolate-programmes-depends-on-speed-compliance-and-monitoring).

The report argues that TTI can contribute to controlling the epidemic only as part of a wider package of public-health measures. Modelling in the supporting [addendum](https://rs-delve.github.io/addenda/2020/05/27/effectiveness-and-resource-requirements-of-tti-strategies.html) — co-authored by [Andrei Paleyes](/people/andrei-paleyes.html) with Bobby He, Bryn Elesedy, Michael Hutchinson, Sheheryar Zaidi, Guy Harling, Anne Johnson and Yee Whye Teh — finds that adding extra-household contact tracing to distancing and household isolation reduces new infections by roughly 5–15%, with the upper end when the test-and-trace cycle is shortened from five days to three.

Alongside the report, the team released [TTI-Explorer](https://rs-delve.github.io/data_software/2020/05/27/tti-explorer.html), open simulation software (Andrei first author) for exploring TTI strategies and distancing measures. Code is on [GitHub](https://github.com/rs-delve/tti-explorer/). Background is on the [DELVE project page](/projects/delve-data-evaluation-and-learning-for-viral-epidemics.html).
