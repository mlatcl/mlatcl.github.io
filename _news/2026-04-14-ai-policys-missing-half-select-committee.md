On 14 April 2026 Neil Lawrence gave oral evidence to the House of Commons [Business and Trade Select Committee](https://committees.parliament.uk/committee/365/business-and-trade-committee/), opening its inquiry into [Artificial Intelligence, business and the future of the workforce](https://committees.parliament.uk/work/9674/artificial-intelligence-business-and-the-future-of-the-workforce/). He appeared alongside Dame Wendy Hall in the first public session; the [session record](https://committees.parliament.uk/event/26983/formal-meeting-oral-evidence-session/) is on Parliament’s site.

Asked to place the UK in a global AI race, Neil argued that compute targets, hyperscale partnerships and frontier-model stories are supply-side reflexes that mistake inputs for outcomes. What is missing is a demand-side strategy: how nurses, teachers, SMEs, councils and hospitals adopt and adapt tools in their own workflows. A pro-innovation agenda, he told the Committee, is not the same as a pro-big-tech agenda — and powers already available under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act should keep markets contestable for UK entrants.

On 28 April he developed the same case in [AI Policy’s Missing Half](https://www.ai.cam.ac.uk/blog/ai-policys-missing-half/) for ai@cam, including the South Cambridgeshire planning collaboration and the idea of Campus UK — using the country’s dense research geography to share lessons of useful deployment. The argument sits with the group’s [AI Adoption](/projects/ai-adoption.html) programme and with the later FT letter on [workflow-by-workflow deployment](/news/2026-05-12-ft-letter-ai-workflow-by-workflow.html).
