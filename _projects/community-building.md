Machine learning advances through communities as much as through papers. ML@CL
supports meetings and programmes that bring researchers together across
institutions, countries and career stages — and that keep open routes for
publishing and training alive.

## UK AI Conference

The [UK AI Conference](https://uk-ai.org/) series brings AI researchers from
across the UK together for talks and posters. The first meeting, at the Natural
History Museum in May 2023, was organised for the Turing AI Fellows. Later
editions in Birmingham (2024), London (2025) and Nottingham (2026) have been
organised principally by [Christian Cabrera](/people/christian-cabrera.html),
with Neil remaining on the series committee. Proceedings from the series appear
in the [Proceedings of Machine Learning Research](https://proceedings.mlr.press/).

## Data Science Africa

[Data Science Africa](http://datascienceafrica.org/) is a grass-roots network
for training and research in data science and machine learning in African
contexts. Lab members contribute as speakers, programme chairs and advisors —
including the 2025 summer school and workshop in Ibadan, and longer-running
collaboration on data-sharing policy and teaching such as the Machine Learning
Foundations course in Kenya.

## Sorrento Meeting

The Sorrento Meeting continues the spirit of the DALI (Data, Learning and
Inference) workshops: a small, invitation-oriented European meeting with time
for workshops and plenary discussion. Cambridge colleagues have chaired the
2025 and 2026 editions at the Grand Hotel Vesuvio. The 2026 meeting centred on
the future of machine learning publishing and marked twenty years of the
Proceedings of Machine Learning Research.

## Proceedings of Machine Learning Research

[PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/) (originally JMLR Workshop and Conference
Proceedings) publishes machine learning research presented at workshops and
conferences. Neil proposed the series to JMLR in 2006; the first volume,
from the Gaussian Processes in Practice workshop at Bletchley Park, appeared in
March 2007. The series remains a community-run, open route for conference
proceedings, with Neil as a series editor.
